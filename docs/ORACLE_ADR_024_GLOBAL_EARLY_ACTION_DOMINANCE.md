# ADR-024 — First globally optimal 20–23-turn Balut action certificates (Iteration 29)
**Date: 2026-11-02. Accepted research-only, NO production change.** Objective unchanged: maximize global expected FINAL Big Points under all-seven-category adaptive future play across full 28 turns.

## The complete early-game theorem
At the CURRENT TURN'S FINAL roll (r=0), if dice show exactly [6,6,6,6,6], all four Sixes slots are already filled, at least one Balut slot is open, and the Choice 100-point bonus is already impossible (or all Choice slots are filled), then **score Balut=50 is the UNIQUE globally optimal legal current action**. Other filled categories may vary. There is no fitted future assumption, no sampled outcome, no Q* numerical approximation. This is a sufficient-condition family of legal scorecards.

Four deterministic, legally reachable constructed witnesses: Sixes row [6,12,18,24] fully filled, Choice has 1,2,3,4 prior scores of 5 respectively, and all other category slots open. They have 23,22,21,20 turns remaining respectively. When one Choice=5 and three remain, Choice maximum is 5+3*30=95, strictly below its +2 threshold at 100. At r=0 with five sixes, other legal categories Fours/Fives/Straight/FullHouse each score zero; Choice scores 30; Balut scores 50.

## Pathwise, globally optimal proof
For any future adaptive policy pi after CURRENTLY scoring a different category c, construct a nonanticipative future policy piPrime which instead scores Balut50 NOW, then follows pi's virtual-game actions and observed future dice through its FIRST future Balut-scoring turn. On that turn piPrime scores category c on the same dice. Until then piPrime has one extra open c slot and pi uses no Balut, so all same hold and scoring actions remain legal. After the swap, category quotas match and piPrime follows pi. This is feasible for EVERY possible adaptive future strategy, not clairvoyance.
Write B(y) for Balut scored on those future dice y (0..50), C(y) for the swapped category. For c=Fours/Fives/Straight/FullHouse (now zero), terminal Small Points change by 50+C(y)-B(y)>=0; their +2 or four-valid rewards cannot deteriorate by replacing 0 now with >=0 later. For c=Choice (now30), Small Points change by 50+Choice(y)-30-B(y)>=0, because at a later fivekind of face w, Choice(y)=5w and B(y)=5w+20 (difference exactly0); otherwise B(y)=0 and Choice(y)>=5. Choice threshold bonus is fixed dead/closed; Balut successes cannot decline; all other category outcomes unchanged. Final Big Point bonus is monotone in total Small Points. Thus piPrime's terminal Big Points >= pi's on EVERY paired path, so Balut is at least globally optimal.
Strict uniqueness: From any partial hold of less than five dice, P(next fivekind)<=1/6. There are at most 3*(T-1) future dice throws. An all-future-non-fivekind event has conditional probability at least (5/6)^(3*(T-1))>0 under EVERY adaptive future policy. At least one later Balut must be scored zero in pi, while piPrime guaranteed current Balut success, adding >=2 terminal Big Points on that event with no losses elsewhere. Hence for each alternative c, Qstar(s,Balut)-Qstar(s,c) >= 2*(5/6)^(3*(T-1)) > 0, an EXACT RATIONAL conservative strict lower bound. This certifies UNIQUE best current action, NOT exact numeric Qstar or complete runner-up ranking.
